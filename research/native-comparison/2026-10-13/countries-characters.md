# Native versus project country and character history: 2026-10-13

This compares installed game files, Extended Timeline, history YAML/docs, and converter source. The game directory name includes v1.2.5, but the local manifest does not verify the executable patch. Native examples show observed format, not universal engine requirements; history docs describe a broader input contract than the converter implements.

For clarity, “58CE output” means the converter’s generated snapshot under `et-converter/out/58/`, not a native game start date. The existing build log records target `58.2.1`, 353 countries, 20,939 locations, and 229 characters; the verify log records 1,224 dynasties. These are output diagnostics, not a claim that the game treats those counts as requirements. The converter README explicitly calls population, technology level, and current age placeholders. Parent inventory independently records 25 setup files, 6 identical to vanilla and 19 modified; this report focuses on field behavior and does not characterize all modifications.

## What the comparison means for prehistory

A game country is a tag with dated ownership/control of mapped locations; archaeology usually documents sites, settlement clusters, material traditions, or sampled subsistence. The converter creates no country from a site record, and the project methodology cautions that a point-site does not establish province population, ownership, religion, language, or dominant subsistence. Require evidence for political organization and territorial mapping, or keep facts in research.

The character generator can serialize names, dates, relationships, culture/religion identifiers, stats, traits, and dynasties, but archaeology seldom supplies biographies for these fields. A burial supports a burial-pattern record; a named character requires independently supported name, date, identity, and relationships. Genetic relatedness alone does not establish dynasty, succession, or political rank.

The 58CE output provides a different example: Rome’s YAML records dated rulers, and Augustus has a conventional birth date, location, dynasty, stats, religion, and culture. The generated snapshot holds 229 living characters; `gaps.txt` includes unresolved character/dynasty references. These are data/integration gaps, unlike unsupported fields. Inspect exact warnings before adding data; aggregate counts do not justify speculative identities.

Country documentation is broader than output. It lists languages, accepted/tolerated cultures, schools, reforms, privileges, laws, diplomacy, dependencies, regnal numbers, and ruler-history inheritance. The model stores a subset; the generator emits ownership buckets, templates, rank, constant technology, government, ruler/consort, exploration, and capital. An absent output field can mean unsupported conversion, missing data, or copied vanilla behavior. Neither vanilla examples nor project docs establish engine requirements.

The generator copies native files and skips native country tags when generating definitions. A history row therefore does not replace static definitions. A new prehistoric tag also needs collision review across definitions, templates, localization, and map locations. Includes select existing templates; history reforms and privileges are not converted into template internals.
## Prehistoric research started from actionable gaps

Two high-priority gaps emerged: individual-level kinship evidence is liable to be misrepresented as dynasty data, and absence of dated personal identities must not be papered over with invented rulers. Three new, atomic findings were added for Çatalhöyük, without adding a country or character: a published study combining 131 paleogenomes with bioarchaeological and archaeological data reports an early-to-later shift in same-building genetic relatedness; it reports maternal-predominant within-building links and no detected sex-biased mobility; and the Çatalhöyük management plan reports no under-floor burials on the West Mound, while treating off-site burial as an interpretation. The study’s abstract dates East Mound occupation to 7100–5950 BCE; the plan’s West Mound discussion describes the subsequent Chalcolithic mound. These facts can guide a research pipeline’s kinship and burial cautions, but none identifies a named parent, ruler, dynasty, political boundary, or religion. The evidence and source details are in the paired YAML files.

Native and project files establish the field mapping. New archaeological claims use a peer-reviewed paleogenomic abstract, the site management plan, and UNESCO chronology; Frontiers documents competing social interpretations, not a definitive government model.

## Priorities and limitations

Prioritize location-aware research before creating prehistoric tags, and require documented uncertainty for any population model. Implement unsupported converter fields before bulk-filling history YAML. For 58CE, triage exact warning references and validate representative records against sources. This is not a global archaeological survey, full engine-requirement audit, or game-launch test.
